2a:T4ed,Open an ad account that has run for a year without a structure and you can read its history in the names: New Campaign Copy 3, Test_final_FINAL, Retargeting - DO NOT TOUCH. Every paid ads team starts with a naming convention; almost none still have an intact one six months later. The format gets agreed in a shared document, applied cleanly for a week, broken on the first launch under deadline pressure, and quietly abandoned by the end of the quarter. Structure and naming are the same decision made twice: once in how you split campaigns, ad sets and ads, and once in how you label that split. Get the first half wrong and your budget fragments into pieces too small to learn anything. Get the second half wrong and the reporting layer collapses, so you cannot tell what worked even when something did. This guide covers both halves with the product names Meta uses in 2026: the three-level architecture and what each level owns, the account structures that fit different monthly budgets, and a naming system built to survive a fifty-ad Monday. The campaign owns the objective: one objective, such as Sales or Leads. If you use Advantage+ campaign budget, the budget lives here too. The ad set owns who sees the ads, where they appear, and what Meta optimizes toward (the performance goal, such as purchases). If you use ad set budgets, the budget lives at this level instead. Splitting ad sets by placement is the classic example: placement is a delivery detail the ad set level already controls, and giving it its own ad set multiplies your budget fragments for no analytical gain.
