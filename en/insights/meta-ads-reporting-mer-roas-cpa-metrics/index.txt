3e:T703,The antidote is a blended metric. MER, the marketing efficiency ratio, is total revenue divided by total ad spend for the same period across all channels. Shopify's explainer (July 18, 2026) gives the clean example: $200,000 in revenue over $50,000 in marketing spend is a MER of 4, four dollars earned per dollar spent, and it notes Gartner's figure that marketing budgets ran at 7.8 percent of company revenue in 2026, nearly flat from 7.7 percent in 2025, which is exactly the planning conversation MER informs. The AdChat version is smaller and just as clear: $12,000 of shop revenue over $4,000 of total ad spend is a MER of 3.0. The reason MER works is that it attributes nothing: organic, email and word-of-mouth revenue sit in the numerator alongside everything else, so the question it answers is whether the money spent on ads grew the business, not whether Meta's model thinks it did. It cannot be gamed by a longer attribution window, and it drops when ads stop working even if platform ROAS stays flat, because customers Meta would have claimed anyway stop showing up in the real total. AdsRunner's operating habit is the reconciliation: each period, put Meta-reported revenue next to actual back-end revenue and total spend, and treat the gap as the most important number in your Meta reporting, the one Ads Manager will never show you. When Ads Manager claims a 6x ROAS and your MER has not improved, the platform is claiming credit it did not earn. 27Five's action triggers complete the loop: MER improving as you raise spend means the new spend is incremental, keep scaling; MER declining as you raise spend means diminishing returns, optimize first; MER stable while budget shifts between channels means the channels are substituting, not adding, so investigate incrementality.2c:["$","p","4",{"className":"leading-[1.9] text-nova-white/85","children":"$3e"}]

3f:T47f,A MER of 3.0 sounds fine until costs enter. AdChat's arithmetic is the version worth taping next to the dashboard. First, gross margin after product costs and fees: revenue minus cost of goods, payment fees, shipping, packaging and returns; say 55 percent. Second, break-even ROAS is one divided by the margin: at 55 percent, that is 1.82, and any spend returning less than 1.82 times its cost in revenue loses money.
