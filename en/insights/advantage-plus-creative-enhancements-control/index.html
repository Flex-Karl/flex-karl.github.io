Advantage+ Creative Enhancements: What Meta Can Change in Your Ads, and How to Keep Control

Meta's Advantage+ creative can rewrite your ad's text, images and destination, and several enhancements start on by default. The documented controls, the October 28 product browsing switch, and how to test on your own numbers.

Every ad you publish on Facebook or Instagram now carries a second, silent author. Advantage+ creative is Meta's umbrella for automatic changes it can make to your ad's images, video, text and destination when it predicts better performance, and the individual switches under it are called enhancements. Most advertisers meet them the same way: they open an ad that has been running for weeks and find music they never uploaded, text they never wrote, or a tap that no longer leads to their website. This article maps what the enhancements actually are, which ones start on by default, what changed on October 28, 2026, who is responsible when the output is wrong, and how to keep control without giving up the automation that genuinely helps.

The umbrella and its switches

Advantage+ creative is not one feature. It is a collection of separate enhancements, each toggled on or off at the ad level in Ads Manager. Meta's Help Center listed 31 of them as of September 2026, 17 carrying an AI label, and the list keeps growing. The labeled set includes text improvements, text generation, show summaries, enhance CTA, image generation, background generation, translations, video subtitles, music and animation. The unlabeled set includes layout and assembly changes such as flex media, carousel additions, product tags and dynamic overlays.

The label matters less than the mechanism. Some enhancements rearrange what you provided, for example moving a phrase from your primary text into a headline or overlay. Others generate new content: a background that never existed, a summary of your selling points written by a model, a music track composed for your video. Rearrangement can still change meaning, but generation can invent it. When you review an ad, the first question is which mechanism each active enhancement uses.

What starts on without you choosing it

Several enhancements are on by default when you build an ad, and PPC Land reports that default-on behavior for creative enhancements dates back to October 2025. The documented defaults include text improvements when you create the ad, add product tags for traffic and sales campaigns with an eligible catalog, and reveal details over time, show spotlights and show summaries for single image or video ads. Show summaries writes an AI summary of your selling points. Any new ad can therefore start with generated text and generated overlays unless someone switches them off, and a one-time cleanup goes stale with the next launch.

There is also an account-level layer. In Advertising settings, under Creating ads, a Creative features section can carry a Test new creative features checkbox. Accounts with that option checked can have features that are still in testing applied to eligible campaigns. If your account shows it, that is the first thing to uncheck, because it operates above anything you set per ad.

Product browsing: the October 28 switch

The clearest example of how this now works arrived in mid-October 2026. Meta circulated a notice telling advertisers: starting October 28, 2026, we will add product browsing to your existing ads. PPC Land published the full notice on October 15. With product browsing, a person who taps your ad may first see an in-app page on Facebook or Instagram with product details, offers and personalized suggestions, instead of going straight to your website. The notice's opt-out button only worked before October 28. After that date, product browsing is a per-ad setting in the Advantage+ creative and Destination sections, and in Sales campaigns with Website as the conversion location it also appears under Personalized destinations.

Meta attached one number to the change: on average, businesses saw 3% more conversions with in-app product browsing experiences compared to campaigns that did not use it. The footnote, published by PPC Land, says the experiment ran from August 6 to 21, 2026 across roughly 117,700 advertisers in at least three verticals including ecommerce, retail and consumer packaged goods, at more than 99% confidence. What the notice does not say is just as important: it names no conversion event, no attribution window, no cost or revenue figures, and it does not say whether conversions were counted on the advertiser's site or inside Meta's apps. An average also does not tell you how many advertisers did worse.

There is a measurement side effect worth understanding before you judge the feature. Your pixel and your analytics only run on your site. A shopper who browses Meta's in-app page and leaves never appears in your data, so link clicks in Ads Manager and sessions from Meta in your own analytics will drift apart while product browsing is on. If your landing page does the selling, the practical move is to turn product browsing off until you have tested it on numbers you control.

Reading Meta's numbers before you trust them

The 3% claim follows a pattern. Meta measures conversions that Meta attributes, on a window it has not disclosed for this test. Meta's Q3 2026 prepared remarks say nearly 10 million advertisers used its generative AI creative tools in the quarter, but adoption is not incremental revenue, and a conversion Meta credits is not a sale that would not have happened anyway. That gap between credited conversions and caused conversions is what an incrementality test measures, and no platform-published average answers it for your account.

Who is responsible when the output is wrong

You are, and that is already in writing. Meta's Ad Creative Generative AI Terms, in place since May 6, 2024, cover features including text generation and text improvements, and state that if you use or publish AI output you are solely responsible for evaluating it and determining its accuracy, suitability, completeness and appropriateness. Meta has proposed going further: Adweek reported on October 22, 2026 that revised Commercial Terms would make advertisers solely responsible for whatever its AI features create, including enhancements Meta applies without being asked. Those terms were due October 30, then moved to November 20, and Reuters reported that Meta's consultation could run past that date. Meta said its existing terms remain in place until then. The practical read does not depend on the final wording: a discount you are not running or a claim you would not make still runs under your brand.

The reported failures are concrete. Adweek described the agency Exverus pulling live ads after Meta's tools added sitelinks, translated copy and generated images from brand assets, sometimes where nobody had switched those features on. Bookshop.org found an AI-generated illustration added to a human-designed ad after an automatic account update and switched off every AI feature. Ads Uploader's guide recalls True Classic in 2025 finding generated images showing a product the brand does not sell. These are the vendor's tools acting inside the advertiser's name, which is why the audit below is not optional hygiene.

Where the controls actually live

Meta says enhancements can be turned off at any time in Ads Manager, and the controls exist, but they are spread across three levels and some of them fight back. Independent guides from Jon Loomer, Opteo and JumpFly, along with agency reports, converge on the same map:

Two traps deserve their own warning. First, some toggles are linked in Meta's backend: Opteo warns that turning off Visual touch-ups also turns off Text improvements, so verify the pair after any change rather than assuming each switch is independent. Second, enhancements can come back. JumpFly reports that swapping a creative, changing a format, duplicating an ad or editing the setup can re-tick recommended enhancements, which is why serious teams log what they changed and when, then re-check after every edit.

Which enhancements deserve which treatment

Here we move from documentation to our own practice, and we mark it as interpretation rather than platform fact. The line we draw is not AI versus non-AI; it is who gets to change what a shopper reads, sees priced, or taps. Anything that writes or rewrites language, such as text improvements, enhance CTA, show summaries, enhance media text and translations, goes off by default in accounts we manage, because a wrong claim or a wrong price runs under the client's name and the client answers for it. Image generation and background generation go off wherever the product must look exactly as it ships. Brightness and contrast changes go off on brand-approved creative, because the brand never approved Meta's version.

At the other end, enhancements that change layout rather than content, such as flex media or carousel card highlighting, are the lowest risk of the set, though they still go through testing before they stay on. The most interesting candidate for catalogs in good shape is add catalog items, which shows products from your catalog beneath the creative you approved: the approved asset stays intact and the products sit below it. Product browsing itself belongs in the test-after-the-peak category for most accounts, strongest where the catalog is clean and the range is broad.

How to test on your own numbers

Whatever you keep, test it the same way you would test any variable: one enhancement at a time, against a holdout, for at least two full weeks so weekday swings even out. Split comparable campaigns or ad sets with budgets, audiences and creative matched, and for a cleaner read split by region the way a geo holdout works. Then read site-side numbers first: sessions from Meta per link click, on-site conversion rate, orders and new-customer revenue. Read the account second: marketing efficiency ratio and contribution margin. Keep an enhancement only if it moves new-customer revenue or MER at the account level. A lift in Meta-attributed conversions with flat orders is credit, not growth.

What this means for a small business

If you run your own ads, the minimum viable routine is short. Once a month, and after any major edit, open your three highest-spend ads, check the Advantage+ creative section and Advanced preview, check the destination settings, and preview each ad on a phone in Feed, Stories and Reels. Keep a simple log of what was on and what you changed. If an enhancement ever puts words or prices in front of customers that you did not approve, turn it off first and investigate second. And before any high-stakes period such as a seasonal sale, run the full account-level audit rather than the sample.

This is also the kind of account work a professional team should simply do for you. In our media buying engagements at NOVA.innovation, enhancement audits sit inside the launch checklist and the monthly review, precisely because defaults reset and notices like the October 28 one arrive with deadlines. The goal is never to switch automation off for its own sake. It is to make sure that every change running under your name is a change you chose, tested on numbers you control.
