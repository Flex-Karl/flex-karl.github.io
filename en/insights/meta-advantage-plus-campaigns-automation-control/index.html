Meta Advantage+ Campaigns: What Meta Automates and What You Still Control

Meta rebuilt Advantage+ in 2025-2026: no more separate automated campaign type, three pre-ticked settings decide automation, and even placement exclusions have a 5 percent allowance. The documented mechanics.

Advantage+ is the word Meta uses for its automated advertising products, and between 2025 and 2026 Meta quietly rebuilt how those products work. Most advice online still describes the old setup: a separate automated campaign type you chose instead of a manual one. That choice no longer exists. This article collects what Meta's developer documentation and changelog actually say about Advantage+ sales campaigns today, what the system automates, which guardrails remain yours, and where the documented limits are. Practitioner interpretation is labeled as such.

The rename that changed the mechanics

Meta's developer documentation now describes Advantage+ sales campaigns as updated, streamlined versions of Advantage+ shopping campaigns and Advantage+ app campaigns. The rename reflects, in Meta's words, the full range of advertisers that can benefit from the product. But the deeper change is structural. The old Advantage+ shopping campaign was a separate choice at campaign creation: automated or manual. That prompt is gone. Every Sales campaign now starts in the same standard flow, and it earns Advantage+ status only while three settings stay on: Advantage+ campaign budget, Advantage+ audience, and Advantage+ placements. Turn any one of the three off and the campaign reports its advantage state as disabled.

So there is no longer a wall between automated and manual. There is a dial with three notches, and Meta sets all three to on by default.

How we got here: a short timeline

Meta launched the Advantage suite in August 2022, when Advantage+ shopping campaigns arrived as an automated, catalog-driven format aimed at ecommerce. Through 2023 and 2024 Meta extended the Advantage+ name across the account: Advantage+ audience replaced the older detailed targeting expansion options, Advantage+ placements folded automatic placements into the same family, and Advantage+ creative began applying automatic enhancements at the ad level. The 2025 and 2026 changes collected these pieces into a single campaign experience: the separate ASC format was retired, the Advantage+ label became a state a campaign holds rather than a type you pick, and manual and automated creation merged into one flow.

The direction is consistent across four years of releases: decisions move from explicit advertiser settings into Meta's prediction systems, and each release defaults more of them to on. Advertisers who learned the 2022 product and have not re-read the documentation since are often running on assumptions the product no longer matches.

The deprecation timeline in Meta's changelog

Meta's Graph API changelog for v24.0 states that beginning with v24.0, creation, duplication and updates to Advantage+ shopping campaigns and Advantage+ app campaigns are no longer allowed. Meta's developer blog gave Q1 2026 for v25.0, when legacy creation is blocked across all API versions. If you run campaigns through a third party tool, this is the reason old templates and duplicated ASC campaigns stopped working: the format they target was retired at the API level, not just renamed in the interface.

What Advantage+ audience does to your targeting

Meta's Marketing API reference describes Advantage+ audience as creating the broadest possible audience to search within. The practical documented behavior: the interests, demographics and custom audience inputs you provide act as a starting point for delivery, and the system can go beyond them when it predicts better performance. Meta notes that non-negotiable business constraints are not expanded.

Which constraints count as non-negotiable? The documented guardrails you keep are location, minimum age, language and audience exclusions. Everything else in the targeting panel is, in the current product, closer to a suggestion than a fence.

Two related changes shrink the panel further. Detailed targeting exclusions were removed in January 2025, and since mid 2025 Meta has been grouping niche interests into broader categories. If your old playbook depended on excluding a narrow interest cluster, that lever no longer exists in this form.

The placement exclusion that is not fully excluded

One documented behavior surprises even experienced buyers. In Sales and Leads campaigns, excluding a placement brings up a pre-ticked box that lets Meta spend up to 5 percent of the budget on each excluded placement. The allowance applies per placement, not in total, so with four placements excluded, up to 20 percent of spend can still go to places you tried to avoid. If a placement is truly unacceptable for your business, an exclusion with this box left ticked is not the guarantee it looks like; you need to untick it deliberately.

Automation is now the starting point, not an option

Across Sales, App and Leads objectives, campaigns are created with Advantage+ switched on, and in early 2026 Meta merged manual and automated campaign creation into a single flow. Manual setup still exists, but it is something you assemble by switching the three Advantage+ settings off one by one. Agencies working daily in Ads Manager describe the same pattern from the inside: automation arrives pre-ticked, and manual control is something you have to go looking for.

What Meta claims about performance, and how to read it

Meta's own testing has reported an average 32 percent ROAS lift for Advantage+ shopping setups over business-as-usual configurations. Treat this as the vendor's account of its own product: directional, averaged across many accounts, and measured against the advertiser's previous setup rather than against a well run manual campaign. Meta also reports very large adoption numbers for Advantage+ sales and shopping campaigns, which say the product is widely used, not that it wins in every account.

The data appetite problem

Automation is hungry. The same learning-phase documentation we covered in our previous article applies here: an ad set needs roughly 50 optimization events in the week after its last significant edit to deliver stably, and Advantage+ concentrates that requirement rather than removing it. An account too new to have conversion history feeds the system nothing to learn from, and the automation punishes a starved account harder than a manual setup, because there is no human steering while the signal is thin. This is the single most important documented constraint for small businesses considering Advantage+.

What manual structure still buys you

A manual campaign hands you direct control over exact audience definitions, individual ad set budgets, placements and exclusions. That control matters in documented situations: when exclusions are non-negotiable for legal or brand reasons, when you launch a product the pixel has never seen and the system has no signal about it, when you must enforce a hard geographic or budget split, and when you are testing a specific hypothesis about who should see which creative. Automation trades these levers for discovery: it can find efficient combinations you would not have tested, but it cannot respect a constraint it does not have.

Advantage+ creative: automation at the ad level

The same family includes Advantage+ creative, which applies automatic enhancements to individual ads: image and video adjustments, format variations and, increasingly, generated text variations. It is controlled per ad, separately from the three campaign-level settings, which means a campaign can show advantage state as disabled at the top while its ads still carry automated enhancements underneath. When you audit an account, check both levels: the campaign's advantage state and each ad's enhancement settings. Agencies report cases where an ad the team believed was fully manual had been quietly running with enhancements applied.

What this means for agencies and API tools

The v24.0 deprecation has a concrete consequence beyond Ads Manager: any third party tool, script or agency workflow that creates or updates Advantage+ shopping or app campaigns through the Marketing API stopped working when its app moved to v24.0, and all versions lose that ability at v25.0 in Q1 2026. Tools that survive are the ones rebuilt around the new Advantage+ sales campaign structure, where automation is expressed as the three settings rather than as a campaign type. If a vendor's workflow suddenly fails or silently creates standard campaigns instead of automated ones, check which API version it calls before assuming your account is broken.

Reading the reporting honestly

Advantage+ sales campaigns blend prospecting and existing-customer delivery inside one campaign, and the headline numbers blend them too. Meta provides an existing-customer budget cap setting that limits how much of the campaign budget can go to people already in your customer lists, which is the documented way to keep an automated campaign pointed at new customers. Without a cap set and checked, a strong return on ad spend can quietly mean the system found the people who were going to buy anyway. Practitioners who audit these campaigns report the same habit: split reporting by audience segment before crediting the automation, and compare against a holdout or a manual control when the spend justifies it. Meta's own case studies are vendor material; your account's segmented reporting is evidence.

How we read this as operators

This section is our interpretation, not Meta's documentation. The hybrid pattern practitioners converge on matches the documented mechanics: use Advantage+ for broad prospecting when you have steady conversion volume, a broad catalog and enough fresh creative to feed the system, and keep manual structure for retargeting segments, protected audiences and controlled tests. Validate angles in a controlled manual test, then hand proven winners to automation to scale. The 2026 defaults mean the decision is no longer whether to use Advantage+; it is which of its three notches you can afford to leave on.

The second practical reading: audit the pre-ticked boxes. The product direction is clear from the changelog and the merged creation flow: Meta is moving decisions from your panel into its system, and the documented 5 percent placement allowance shows that even its exclusions are negotiated. A monthly habit of checking advantage state, placement breakdowns and actual audience delivery against what you intended is now part of running Meta ads, not an advanced extra.

What this means for a small business in the Gulf

For a small store in Saudi Arabia or Egypt spending a few hundred dollars a month, the documented constraints point one way. Advantage+ can work once the account has steady purchase or lead volume and several fresh creatives each month; before that, a manual campaign with one or two ad sets and careful measurement gives you control the algorithm cannot yet replace. And whichever you run, connect it to the learning phase math from our previous article: every campaign you add splits the budget into another pool that needs its own roughly 50 results a week.
