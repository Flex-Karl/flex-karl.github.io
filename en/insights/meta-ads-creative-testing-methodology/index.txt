One ad changed the concept, the creator and the hook at once, so its loss could not say which part failed. Ten near-copies of one idea got logged as ten tests. Different means a different reason to buy.2a:T4fb,The core of the Datadrew methodology is four levels of testing, and the rule that a test only teaches you about the level it changed. Level A, the concept, is the reason to buy: problem and solution versus aspiration, demonstration versus social proof, founder story versus offer. Product, offer and audience stay fixed; if it loses, the argument did not land, so test a different argument and do not spend hook tests trying to rescue it. Level B, the angle or persona, points a proven concept at new people: the same idea aimed at commuters, then at parents. A loss means wrong person for this concept, which can still sell to the people it already works for. Level C, the hook and execution, changes how the ad opens and moves: the first three seconds, the first line of primary text, the creator, the pacing, the order the proof arrives in. Level D is iteration: new cuts of a proven winner, a shorter edit, a new first frame, everything that made it win held fixed. The reading rule binds the whole system: each level can only convict itself. A flopped hook convicts the hook, not the concept; a tired execution convicts that cut, not the angle, until the angle's other executions tire too.
