AI for Business

GPT and Claude, explained for business owners

Two AI assistants can draft your posts, answer customers and organize your week. This page shows what each one does best, ready prompts you can copy, and what the plans cost, in plain language.

The two assistants

Same idea, different strengths

by OpenAI

ChatGPT · The all-rounder

The most widely used AI assistant. Strong at writing, ideas, image generation, voice chats and quick research, with custom GPTs and a large app ecosystem.

Best for: content, marketing and general daily work.

by Anthropic

Claude · The careful writer

Known for natural, careful writing and very long documents. Strong at analysis, structured projects, coding and following detailed instructions exactly.

Best for: long documents, detailed analysis and careful writing.

What you can use it for

Six jobs you can hand to AI this week

Start with one job, review every result before it goes out, and keep what works as a saved prompt.

Social media content

A week of post ideas, captions and hashtags in your brand voice, drafted in minutes.

Customer replies

Polite, fast answers to common questions and complaints, ready for you to review before sending.

Ad copy

Several versions of your headline and ad text to test, tuned to the audience you describe.

Market research

Summaries of what competitors do and what customers keep asking, laid out in a table.

Emails and documents

Quotes, proposals, policies and supplier emails drafted from a few bullet points.

Planning and analysis

Turn scattered sales notes into next week's priorities, tasks and a simple budget.

Ready prompts

Copy, paste, fill the brackets

Replace the [brackets] with your details. The more specific you are, the better the answer.

Weekly content plan

You are a social media manager for a [type of business] in [city]. Write 5 Facebook post ideas for this week: hook, caption and call to action. Tone: friendly and professional. No emojis in 2 of them.

Reply to an unhappy customer

A customer wrote: [paste their message]. Write a short, polite reply that apologizes, says what we will do next, and asks for one missing detail. Do not admit fault beyond what I state here.

Ad variations to test

Write 5 versions of a Facebook ad for [product or service]: a headline under 40 characters, primary text under 125 characters, and one clear call to action. Audience: [describe your audience].

Notes into a plan

Here are my notes from today: [paste notes]. Turn them into: 1) decisions made, 2) tasks with owners, 3) the three priorities for next week.
